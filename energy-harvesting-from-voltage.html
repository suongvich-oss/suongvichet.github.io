Energy harvesting note

How to calculate power and harvested energy from measured voltage

A practical measurement note for piezoelectric and self-powered sensing experiments.

The main idea

Voltage is the easiest signal to measure, but voltage alone is not power. To calculate power, I need to know where the voltage was measured: across a load resistor, across a storage capacitor, or in open circuit.

If the voltage is measured across a known load resistor, the most useful equation is: P_avg = mean(v(t)^2) / R_load.

Useful quantities from the voltage waveform

  • V_max: highest voltage in the selected time window.
  • V_min: lowest voltage in the selected time window.
  • V_pp = V_max - V_min: peak-to-peak voltage.
  • V_rms = sqrt(mean(v(t)^2)): RMS voltage.
  • P_avg = V_rms^2 / R_load: average power through the load resistor.

Energy delivered to a resistor

For time-series data, calculate instantaneous power at every sample: p_i = v_i^2 / R_load. Then integrate it over time: E = sum(p_i * dt_i).

This is better than using only peak-to-peak voltage because real experimental signals can be pulsed, noisy, or non-sinusoidal.

Energy stored in a capacitor

If the harvester charges a capacitor, the stored energy is: E_cap = 0.5 * C * (V_end^2 - V_start^2).

The average charging power is: P_avg = E_cap / charging_time.

My reporting checklist

  1. State the time window used for analysis.
  2. State the load resistor or capacitor value.
  3. Report peak-to-peak voltage and RMS voltage.
  4. Calculate average power from the waveform.
  5. Compare multiple loads to find maximum power, not only maximum voltage.
